A question for families with ages 8–15

What if they could practice a next step?

When a friendship or school moment gets hard, knowing what to do can take practice. We are exploring short, private fictional scenarios with parents and caregivers of 8–15-year-olds. The existing prototypes are for ages 8–12; what would work for teens is still an open question.

Our design promise
Made for real families

A calmer kind of early access.

We are building this with parents, not around them. The first group will help us decide what belongs in the game — and what does not.

For grown-ups

Tell us what would feel useful, awkward, or inappropriate for your family. A current 8–12 prototype can illustrate the concept; no sensitive stories are required.

For young people

Our 8–12 prototypes use fictional, private choices without grades or personal disclosures. For ages 13–15, we are listening first: older teens deserve their own voice, scenarios, and visual style, not a relabeled younger-child game.

Clear answers

Good questions belong here.

Will my child talk to strangers?

No. The prototype has no open chat, public profiles, child-to-child matching, or public social feed.

Is this therapy or safeguarding support?

No. It is an early product experiment about guided practice. It is not therapy, crisis support, safeguarding advice, or a promise to stop bullying.

What happens after I join?

Adults can sign up for updates and indicate interest in an optional parent conversation. Any prototype testing would require a separate invitation and suitability review, especially for ages 13–15.

What information do you collect?

Only what is needed for the waitlist and optional research contact. Please do not send a child’s name or a sensitive personal story through this page.
